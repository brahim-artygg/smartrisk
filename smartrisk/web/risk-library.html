Know the signals before you read the score.

These are the kinds of issues SmartRisk looks for. Seeing a signal does not automatically mean a project is malicious; the context matters.

Honeypot and sell restrictions

A token may allow buys while making sells fail, revert, or behave very differently. SmartRisk checks trading paths where the available infrastructure allows it.

Owner permissions

Some contracts leave powerful functions with the owner or another privileged role. Examples include pausing transfers, changing fees, blacklisting addresses, minting tokens, or replacing the implementation behind a proxy.

Liquidity

Low or fragile liquidity can make a token difficult to exit even when transfers technically work. SmartRisk looks at available liquidity and related pool signals.

Holder concentration

If a small number of wallets control a large share of supply, those wallets can have a large effect on price and market depth. Concentration is a market-structure signal, not proof of wrongdoing.

Taxes and transfer behavior

Some tokens charge transfer or trading fees, and some contracts change those fees later. SmartRisk checks for unusually high, dynamic, or asymmetric behavior when it can observe it.

Proxy and upgradeability

Upgradeable contracts can change logic after deployment. That is not automatically bad, but it is important to know who can upgrade the contract and what controls exist around that power.
